Timing boundaries

Complete-program rows include startup, input preparation, computation, transfers and output. Each cell is a median of ten correctness-gated executions after warmup. Repeated-request rows exclude startup and use medians of three process means, with ten measured pricing requests or 30 game-search batches after two warmups. Quote and game-search timings end when their results are available on the CPU.

Relative time divides each cell by the fastest measured time in its row. Lower is better. Compare implementations within a row, not ratios across different workloads. Highlighting is descriptive, not a significance test.

Comparison implementations

Published workloads use serial C and project-written OpenMP, with project CUDA for Game of Life. BOTS uses its canonical OpenMP tasks. CUB uses serial C++ controls and library sorting/reduction. HotSpot uses corrected Rodinia OpenMP and CUDA pyramid height 1. BFS uses GAP and Gunrock. Pricing and game search use project-written OpenMP/CUDA. Bit-mask N-Queens uses serial C++ and OpenMP tasks through row 3.

Sorting algorithms and data representations differ. The two game-search rows are separate runs; missing CPU measurements are not filled from another run. Failed, invalid and superseded measurements, kernel-only timings and the separate reduction crossover sweep are excluded.
